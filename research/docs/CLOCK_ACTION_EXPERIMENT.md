# Experiment 5: action-derived clock–field interaction

## Question

Can a dynamical clock coordinate couple to a field through a stationary-action
model, rather than through the externally injected source used in Experiment 3?

## Model ansatz

Let q collect the existing scalar, phase, and tensor-array generalized
coordinates, with scalar coordinates x_i. Define the scalar-field observable

    C(x) = 1/2 * sum_i x_i^2 / (1 + x_i^2).

Introduce a clock coordinate T with positive inertia I_T and restoring
coefficient kappa. The augmented Lagrangian is

    L(q,qdot,T,Tdot)
      = 1/2 qdot^T M qdot + 1/2 I_T Tdot^2
        - V(q) - 1/2 kappa T^2 + g T C(x).

Equivalently, the augmented potential is

    U_aug(q,T) = V(q) + 1/2 kappa T^2 - g T C(x).

The Euler–Lagrange equations for constant diagonal field inertia M are

    M qddot = -grad_q V(q) + g T grad_q C(x)
    I_T Tddot = -kappa T + g C(x).

For the scalar coordinates, partial C / partial x_i = x_i / (1 + x_i^2)^2. The bounded observable keeps the clock–field interaction potential bounded below for positive clock stiffness, avoiding an unbounded cubic coupling. Other field
coordinates receive no direct coupling in this first prototype, though they
may remain coupled through V(q).

## Hamiltonian and energy

The canonical Hamiltonian is

    H = 1/2 p^T M^-1 p + P_T^2/(2 I_T)
        + V(q) + 1/2 kappa T^2 - g T C(x).

The model is autonomous. Therefore its continuous equations conserve H.
The implemented velocity-Verlet method should show bounded, timestep-dependent
energy error rather than exact discrete conservation.

## Numerical protocol

The example compares g=0 (uncoupled control) with g=0.1, using identical
initial field and clock states. Both systems are integrated to lambda=4
with dt=0.002. Diagnostics include clock coordinate, total Hamiltonian,
and field-state separation.

The test suite checks energy-error bounds, the zero-coupling limit, a
nonzero-coupling response, and basic parameter validation.

## What this establishes

This experiment demonstrates that a clock coordinate can be included as an
ordinary generalized coordinate in a common action/Hamiltonian model. The
clock–field coupling is generated by the shared potential and changes both
clock and field equations. It is not merely a relabeling of an external
parameter.

## Limits

The coupling g T C(x) is a selected ansatz, not derived from a deeper OTFT
principle or established physical law. All coordinates and coefficients are
dimensionless prototypes. The clock coordinate is a dynamical degree of
freedom, not automatically the physical proper time measured by an ideal
clock. The scalar amplitude C is not the derivative-based activity A used
in Experiments 1–4. The model has not been fitted to observations or compared
against experimental bounds.

A physical theory would require unit assignments, a covariant field action,
well-defined spacetime geometry, operational clock observables, stability
analysis, and empirical predictions that distinguish it from existing
theories.
